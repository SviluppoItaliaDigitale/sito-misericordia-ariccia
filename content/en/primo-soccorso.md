*[‹ English home](/en/)*

> **In an emergency always call [112](tel:112).** These notes are a basic reminder and **do not replace** a first-aid course or the intervention of rescuers. Always follow the 112 operator's instructions.

Knowing **what to do in the first few minutes** can make the difference. Here are essential pointers for the most common emergencies. To gain real skills, take a [first-aid and BLSD course](/en/formazione/) with the Misericordia.

## Golden rules

1. **Stay calm** and make the scene safe for you and the casualty (traffic, electricity, gas).
2. **Call 112**: say where you are, what happened, how many people and their condition. Don't hang up until told to.
3. **Do not move** the person unless in immediate danger, especially after trauma.
4. **Stay with them** and keep talking until help arrives.

## Common emergencies

- **Cardiac arrest (no response, not breathing normally — rare, noisy gasps are not normal breathing):** call 112 on speaker and ask for a defibrillator (AED), start **chest compressions** in the centre of the chest, 5–6 cm deep and fast (100–120/min). If trained, 30 compressions to 2 rescue breaths; if not, compressions only. Follow the AED's voice prompts.
- **Choking (cannot breathe, speak or cough):** **call 112 straight away** (or have someone call), then give up to **5 back blows** between the shoulder blades and up to **5 abdominal thrusts** (Heimlich); alternate until cleared, and never sweep blindly with your fingers. If they collapse, start compressions, and call 112 if nobody has yet. Anyone who has had abdominal thrusts must be checked by a doctor. *Babies under 1 year: 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts with two thumbs — never abdominal thrusts.*
- **Fainting:** lay the person on their back and, if you can, **raise their legs**; if she is pregnant, lay her **on her left side** instead. If they don't come round straight away, call 112 and check their breathing: if it is normal and they did not hit themselves hard when falling, put them **on their side**; if it is not normal, start CPR. Help them up slowly once they feel better. Call 112 if they fainted during exertion, with chest pain or palpitations, or were hurt in the fall.
- **Severe bleeding:** press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth; don't remove it if soaked — add another on top; call 112 for heavy bleeding.
- **Burn:** cool under **running cool water for at least 20 minutes** (not ice-cold); remove rings and clothing unless stuck to the skin; don't apply ice, butter, oil or creams and don't burst blisters; cover loosely with cling film or a clean dressing; call 112 if it is large or deep, on the face, hands, feet or genitals, caused by flames, electricity or chemicals, or in a child; for an older person, or if in doubt, see a doctor.
- **Heatstroke:** very hot skin with confusion, agitation, seizures or unresponsiveness: call 112 and start cooling them at once. Move them to shade or a cool place and remove as much clothing as possible; lie them down if they feel dizzy, on their side if they feel sick. Wet their whole body with cool water or wrap them in wet sheets and keep fanning them, changing the part of the body exposed; when the sheets dry, wet them again. Keep going until help arrives. No bath, cold shower or ice. Small sips of water only if fully awake and not nauseous; no alcohol rubs and no fever medicines; if they don't respond and aren't breathing normally, start CPR.
- **Seizures:** move dangerous objects away, put something soft under the head, don't hold them down and put nothing in their mouth. When the jerking stops, check their breathing: if it is normal, put them on their side; if not, call 112 and start CPR. Call 112 if it lasts more than 5 minutes, is the first seizure, they are injured or pregnant, seizures repeat or they don't come round. If in doubt, call.
